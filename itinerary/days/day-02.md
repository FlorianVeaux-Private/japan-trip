## Day 2 — Oct 2 — Tsukiji, Asakusa, Kappabashi and goshuincho

**Sleep:** Tokyo

This is the east Tokyo food, craft and traditional-atmosphere day. It gives a strong first taste of old Tokyo without making the day only about temples.

### Plan

- **Tsukiji Outer Market** in the morning  
- Optional garden break after Tsukiji  
- Move toward Asakusa  
- **Senso-ji**  
- Buy a **goshuincho** if a good one is available  
- **Kappabashi** kitchenware street  
- Casual dinner

### Recommended skeleton

**Suggested flow:**

1. Tsukiji breakfast / food grazing  
2. Hamarikyu Garden if the weather is good  
3. Asakusa / Senso-ji  
4. Buy goshuincho  
5. Kappabashi kitchenware street  
6. Kuramae, Ueno, or Ameyoko if energy remains  
7. Casual dinner

### Filler options depending on mood

- **Hamarikyu Garden** — calm contrast after Tsukiji; good if the day starts early and the weather is pleasant.  
- **Sumida River walk** — easy, scenic filler around Asakusa.  
- **Tokyo Skytree from a distance** — nice visual anchor from the Asakusa/Sumida area; no need to go up by default.  
- **Kuramae** — cafés, stationery, small craft/design shops; calmer than Omotesando and more aligned with the craft/shopping mood.  
- **Ueno / Ameyoko** — lively market streets, useful if you want more food and street energy.  
- **Tokyo National Museum** — only if you want a serious cultural/museum add-on; otherwise save energy.  
- **Extra casual food stops** — sushi, tempura, soba, melonpan, taiyaki, matcha sweets, or convenience-store dessert scouting.

### Goshuincho note

This is a good day to start the goshuin notebook. The most natural option is to buy one at **Senso-ji** if a design feels right. If not, keep an eye out in Asakusa/Kuramae shops or buy one later at Meiji Jingu.
