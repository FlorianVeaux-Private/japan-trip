# Japan 5-Week Itinerary — Tokyo to Busan

**Dates:** October 1 to November 7  
**Direction:** Tokyo arrival → Fukuoka / Hakata ferry departure to Busan  
**Travel style:** traditional culture, food, beautiful nature, old roads, light hiking, limited rural complexity

---

## Overview

This itinerary is built around a first trip to Japan that includes the classic cultural core, but avoids being only the Golden Route. It combines Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka and Nara with Nikko, the Kiso Valley, Takayama, Kanazawa, Koyasan, a Kumano Kodo taster, Kinosaki Onsen, Onomichi, the Shimanami Kaido, Matsuyama, Hiroshima, Miyajima, and Fukuoka before continuing by ferry to Busan.

The trip is full but coherent. The most logistically demanding stretch is the middle-to-late section:

**Koyasan → Nakahechi / Hongu → Shingu / Hayatama → Nachi / Kii-Katsuura → Osaka → Kinosaki Onsen → Onomichi → Shimanami Kaido → Matsuyama → Hiroshima → Miyajima → Fukuoka → Busan ferry**

This is also one of the most distinctive parts of the trip. There is an Osaka recovery block after Kumano, then Kinosaki as a one-night onsen-town reset before the Shimanami cycling section. Matsuyama is a one-night Shikoku landing after the ride: enough time for Matsuyama Castle and the Seto Inland Sea ferry. Fukuoka is an afternoon and evening before the Nov 7 ferry to Busan.

In Kumano, the route completes the Kumano Sanzan by walking the Nakahechi to Hongu, then visiting Hayatama Taisha in Shingu and giving Nachi its own day from a two-night Kii-Katsuura base.

The trip finishes westward — Shimanami Kaido → Matsuyama → Hiroshima / Miyajima → Fukuoka → Busan ferry — without backtracking to Osaka.

# Day-by-day itinerary

## Day 1 — Oct 1 — Arrive in Tokyo / Shinjuku soft landing

**Sleep:** Tokyo  
**Lodging type:** comfortable city hotel in a practical area.

Arrive in Tokyo around **8:00am**. Keep this as an acclimatization day, but not an empty one. The goal is to get oriented, spend time outside, eat something easy, and avoid making the first night miserable.

### Plan

- Arrive at the airport  
- Transfer into Tokyo  
- Drop bags at the hotel  
- Get cash / IC card / basic logistics sorted  
- Easy lunch near the hotel or Shinjuku  
- **Shinjuku Gyoen**  
- Shinjuku wander  
- Casual dinner  
- Early-ish night

### Recommended skeleton

Use Shinjuku as the main zone for the day.

**Suggested flow:**

1. Airport → hotel / bag drop  
2. Simple lunch  
3. Shinjuku Gyoen  
4. Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building observatory if energy allows  
5. Depachika food basement or Omoide Yokocho for dinner  
6. Early night

### Filler options depending on mood

- **Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building observatory** — easy skyline view, good if the weather is clear.  
- **Isetan or Takashimaya depachika** — excellent food basement browsing; good for snacks, sweets, or low-pressure dinner.  
- **Omoide Yokocho** — atmospheric alley near Shinjuku Station; good for a short look or simple bite.  
- **Kabukicho / Godzilla head** — quick “this is Tokyo” moment, not worth over-planning.  
- **Golden Gai quick walk** — visually interesting, but better as a quick look than a drinking night on arrival.  
- **Meiji Jingu only if unusually energetic** — possible, but better saved for Day 3 unless the day is going very smoothly.

### Notes

Do not plan teamLab, Akihabara, Kabuki, or a major dinner reservation on arrival day. Jet lag may hit later than expected.

### Filler options depending on mood

## Day 3 — Oct 3 — Meiji Jingu, Shibuya and Nintendo TOKYO

**Sleep:** Tokyo

This day combines a forested shrine morning with modern/pop-culture Tokyo. Since fashion is not a major interest, skip Harajuku and Omotesando as destinations. They can simply function as transit areas if needed.

### Plan

- **Meiji Jingu** in the morning  
- Skip or only lightly pass through Harajuku  
- Move to Shibuya  
- **Nintendo TOKYO** at Shibuya PARCO  
- Optional Pokémon Center Shibuya if convenient, but no Pokémon Café

### Recommended skeleton

**Suggested flow:**

1. Meiji Jingu  
2. Shibuya crossing / Hachiko quick look  
3. Shibuya PARCO  
4. Nintendo TOKYO  
5. Optional Pokémon Center Shibuya  
6. Shibuya Sky, Daikanyama, Nakameguro, or Shimokitazawa depending on mood  
7. Casual dinner

### Filler options depending on mood

- **Shibuya crossing / Hachiko** — touristy but very quick and iconic.  
- **Shibuya Sky** — strong paid skyline option if the weather is clear and you want one city-view experience.  
- **Miyashita Park** — easy modern urban filler near Shibuya.  
- **Daikanyama** — calmer cafés, bookstores, design shops; more grown-up and relaxed than Harajuku.  
- **Nakameguro** — canal-side walking, cafés, dinner options; good if you want a softer evening.  
- **Shimokitazawa** — young/casual neighborhood with vintage shops, record shops, cafés, curry and bars; better than Harajuku if you want youth culture without the fashion-tourist chaos.  
- **Food crawl around Shibuya/Ebisu** — ramen, standing sushi, yakitori, izakaya, dessert, or depachika-style grazing.

### Notes

If the goshuincho was not bought on Day 2, **Meiji Jingu** is another strong place to buy one and start the stamp journey.

This day should not be fashion-focused. Harajuku and Omotesando are optional pass-throughs only.

**Booking note — Nintendo TOKYO:** Oct 3 is a **Saturday**, when Nintendo TOKYO at Shibuya PARCO uses a free **same-day numbered/timed-entry ticket** system. Tickets are handed out from roughly **8:00–9:30am** at the ground-floor entrance and can run out. Options: send one person to grab a ticket at \~8am while the other starts Meiji Jingu, do it on a weekday instead, or try walk-in entry in the evening. There is no online reservation.

Walk the preserved Nakasendo section in the traditional direction — mostly downhill, \~8km, 2-3 hours at a comfortable pace.

| Time |  |

**Why this is a key module:**  
It gives traditional architecture, old-road history and forest walking without making the trip a trekking expedition.

# Takayama

## Day 10 — Oct 10 — Kiso Valley → Takayama (Hachiman Matsuri Day 2\)

**Sleep:** Takayama  
**Lodging type:** machiya-style hotel, comfortable small hotel, or ryokan-style stay.

### Travel: Kiso Valley to Takayama

| Time |  |
| :---- | :---- |
| \~07:52 | Shinano Limited Express departs Nakatsugawa → Nagoya (\~50 min) |
| \~08:42 | Arrive Nagoya. Transfer to Hida Limited Express. |
| \~09:43 | Hida Limited Express departs Nagoya → Takayama (\~2h22) |
| \~12:05 | Arrive Takayama |

The Hida runs roughly hourly from Nagoya at :43 — take the **09:43 rather than the 10:43** to avoid a \~2-hour idle gap in Nagoya station and gain an extra hour of festival time. Reserve seats in advance. (Verify the exact 09:43 departure against the 2026 timetable.)

### Afternoon: Hachiman Matsuri Day 2

Arriving around **noon** puts you in Takayama while the festival is still active.

- **Yatai float procession** runs through the Sanmachi district from \~9am; floats remain displayed through the afternoon  
- **Karakuri puppet performances** — mechanical dolls performing on top of the floats; there are morning and afternoon sessions, so an early-afternoon arrival still catches one  
- Float display winds down in the late afternoon

**Be clear about what a Day-2 afternoon arrival misses:** the festival is front-loaded to Oct 9\. You get floats on display plus (likely) one afternoon karakuri session on Oct 10, but you miss the **Oct 9 afternoon yatai parade, the Oct 10 morning karakuri, and the Oct 9 evening lantern parade** (the most atmospheric moment). Still worthwhile, just not the full festival.

### Evening

- Hida beef dinner  
- Early night before the full town day tomorrow

# Kyoto and Uji

> ### ⚠️ Book these Kyoto experiences well ahead (peak autumn)

> The 5 open Kyoto base days are fine as placeholders, but several first-timer highlights are reservation-gated and can't be arranged on the day:  
> 

> - **Saihoji (Kokedera moss temple)** — online reservation opens **exactly 2 months ahead** (≈ Aug 20 for an Oct 20 visit).  
> - **A top kaiseki dinner** — the best places book weeks ahead, more so in foliage season.  
> - **A formal tea ceremony** — reserve ahead.  
> - **Arashiyama bamboo grove** — go at opening to beat crowds (no booking, but timing matters).

> ### 🎮 Nintendo Museum — separate half-day trip (NOT the Uji day)

> - Near **Ogura Station** (between Kyoto and Uji), \~2–4 hours on its own.  
> - **Lottery-only tickets, applied \~3 months ahead** — for an October visit the window is essentially **now (mid-July 2026\)**. If not already entered, enter today or drop it.  
> - **Closed Tuesdays** — so NOT Oct 20 (Day 20). Slot it on another Kyoto base day; a **weekday** (e.g. Mon Oct 19 / Day 19\) is less crowded than the weekend.

# Hiroshima and Miyajima

## Day 36 — Nov 5 — Hiroshima full day

**Sleep:** Hiroshima  
**Lodging type:** comfortable city hotel near Peace Memorial Park or Hiroshima Station.

### Plan

Single-focus day. Do not overload it.

**Suggested flow:**

1. **Peace Memorial Park** in the morning — outdoors, free, most affecting before tour groups arrive  
2. **A-Bomb Dome (Genbaku Dome)** — the surviving shell of the 1915 Industrial Promotion Hall, preserved exactly as the blast left it. Spend time here.  
3. **Peace Memorial Museum** — allow 2.5 to 3 hours. The 2019 renovation made it one of the most effectively curated museums in the world on its subject. ### Filler options depending on mood

- **Hiroshima Castle** — postwar concrete reconstruction (the original was destroyed in 1945, worth knowing). Interior museum covers Hiroshima under the Mori and Fukushima clans. Worth 1–1.5h for castle history interest; not essential for this trip.  
- **Hondori covered arcade** — easy afternoon wander if you need a break after the museum.

### Notes

The Peace Museum is emotionally demanding. The right pace after leaving is a walk, a coffee, and some quiet — not another major sight. Hiroshima as a city is modern outside the park (destroyed and rebuilt from nothing in 1945). The park and museum are the reason to come, and they are more than enough.
